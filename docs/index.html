TL;DR

We learn tractor–trailer dynamics from recent motion to improve control when friction and terrain are unknown. In BeamNG.tech, we test reverse driving at target speeds of 30 and 50 km/h and forward driving at 80 km/h.

Learned MPPI completes 62 of 72 reverse trials, compared with 41 of 72 for analytic MPPI, with the improvement coming from fewer stalls. Both complete all 36 forward trials. The learned controller also transfers to slopes and banking absent from training, though reverse stalls remain a challenge on snowy slopes.

Why tractor–trailers?

Why high-speed reverse driving?

A passive trailer is difficult to stabilize in reverse. Snow and uneven terrain make accurate motion prediction harder.

01

Coupled motion

The tractor controls a passive trailer through the hitch. A steering correction changes both vehicles’ motion.

02

Reverse instability

Small motion errors can grow into a jackknife. Higher speed leaves less time to correct them.

03

Unobserved conditions

Friction, slope, banking, and suspension affect the vehicle’s response but are not direct inputs to the learned model.

Much of tractor–trailer control research focuses on parking and low-speed navigation. We study reverse driving near handling limits, where errors in the dynamics model directly affect control.

03 Method

MPPI with learned dynamics

A neural network predicts vehicle motion from a short state-action history. MPPI uses these predictions to choose steering and throttle/brake commands.

Network

An MLP with four hidden layers of 128 units takes four steps of state-action history.

Inputs and outputs

Hitch angle, velocities, yaw rates, and commanded and observed actuator states predict accelerations and actuator rates. Position and orientation are propagated kinematically.

Training data

Offline trajectories vary speed and friction, with control noise, weaving, and added runs near handling limits.

For comparison, we use a planar tractor–trailer model with Fiala tire forces. The derivation is in the paper.

Simulation settings

Four test environments

We add snow and terrain changes separately to a Barcelona-Catalunya track layout in BeamNG.tech. Slope and banking are absent from training. The same snow pattern and terrain are reused in the combined setting.

04 Results

Closed-loop performance

Each speed and environment has nine trials: three seeds at three starting locations. Completion means reaching 500 control steps (25 seconds), not finishing a lap.

More reliable reverse driving

Learned MPPI completes 62/72 reverse trials versus 41/72 for analytic MPPI, with 21 fewer stalls and one jackknife each.

Better tracking

Mean speeds are closer to the targets and RMS hitch angles are lower than analytic MPPI at every tested setpoint.

Unseen terrain

Learned MPPI completes 16/18 reverse trials on Slope and 10/18 on Snow + Slope. Snowy slopes remain the hardest setting.

Speed tracking and completed runs

The plots show four of the five controllers; Table II includes MPCC and the full results. Both MPPI variants complete all 36 forward trials. LQR–PID has lower forward RMS hitch angles, but fewer completions on Asphalt and Snow.

Dynamics prediction error

Starting from the same measured state, three models replay the same recorded commands for five seconds: a fixed-friction analytic prior, a prior given the recorded contact-friction sequence, and our learned model with four steps of history.

Across the eight selected direction–environment segments, learned dynamics achieve the lowest position RMSE in six and the lowest hitch-angle RMSE in seven. Recorded friction information does not consistently close the analytic model’s prediction gap.

Large late reverse errors remain. This diagnostic horizon exceeds the main controller’s planning horizons of 4.0 seconds forward and 3.25 seconds reverse. See Table III for all RMSE values.
