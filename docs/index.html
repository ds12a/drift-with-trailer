TL;DR

We learn tractor–trailer dynamics from recent motion to improve control when friction and terrain are unknown. In BeamNG.tech, we test reverse driving at target speeds of 30 and 50 km/h and forward driving at 80 km/h.

Learned-dynamics MPPI completes more runs than the tested analytic controllers, with its strongest advantage in reverse. It also transfers to slopes and banking absent from training, though reverse stalls remain a challenge on snowy slopes.

Why tractor–trailers?

Why high-speed reverse driving?

A passive trailer is difficult to stabilize in reverse. Snow and uneven terrain make accurate motion prediction harder.

01

Coupled motion

The tractor controls a passive trailer through the hitch. A steering correction changes both vehicles’ motion.

02

Reverse instability

Small motion errors can grow into a jackknife. Higher speed leaves less time to correct them.

03

Unobserved conditions

Friction, slope, banking, and suspension affect the vehicle’s response but are not direct inputs to the learned model.

Much of tractor–trailer control research focuses on parking and low-speed navigation. We study reverse driving near handling limits, where errors in the dynamics model directly affect control.

02 Contributions

What we evaluate

01

High-speed forward and reverse control

Learned MPPI against Fiala-prior MPPI, LQR-PID, IPOPT-based MPC, and MPCC across four environments.

02

Prediction under uncertainty

Five-second rollouts from learned dynamics and analytic priors with and without access to local friction.

03

Reduced-order analytic dynamics

A coupled tractor–trailer model with Fiala tire forces provides the physical baseline.

03 Method

MPPI with learned dynamics

A neural network predicts vehicle motion from a short state-action history. MPPI uses these predictions to choose steering and acceleration.

Network

An MLP with four hidden layers of 128 units takes four steps of state-action history.

Inputs and outputs

Hitch angle, velocities, yaw rates, and commanded and realized controls predict accelerations and the next realized controls.

Training data

Offline trajectories vary speed and friction, with control noise, weaving, and added runs near handling limits.

Analytic dynamics

The analytic baseline

A planar tractor–trailer model couples both bodies through a rigid hitch and uses Fiala tire forces.

State and configuration

The state includes position, both yaw angles, tractor velocity, and both yaw rates.

Coupled equations of motion

The hitch couples tractor and trailer accelerations. Tire forces and inertial terms are solved together at each step.

Fiala-tire analytic prior

Lateral tire force depends on slip angle, friction, normal load, and cornering stiffness. The model omits many terrain, suspension, and powertrain effects present in BeamNG.tech.

Simulation settings

Four test environments

We add snow and terrain changes separately to a Barcelona-Catalunya track layout in BeamNG.tech. Slope and banking are absent from training. The same snow pattern and terrain are reused in the combined setting.

04 Results

Closed-loop performance

Each speed and environment has nine trials: three seeds at three starting locations. Completion means reaching 500 control steps (25 seconds), not finishing a lap.

Asphalt and Snow

Learned MPPI completes all 27 trials in each environment, across both reverse targets and the forward target.

Unseen slopes

Learned MPPI completes 25/27 trials on Slope: 8/9 at each reverse target and 9/9 forward.

Snowy slopes

Learned MPPI completes 19/27 trials with no jackknife failures. Eight reverse trials end in stalls.
