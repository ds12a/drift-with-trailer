TL;DR

We study the hardest regime for tractor–trailer control: high-speed reverse driving when the vehicle encounters unobserved snow patches, slopes, and banking. Our MLP-based learned dynamics model is used within an MPPI controller to infer these hidden effects from state-action history.

Compared with a Fiala-tire analytic prior and residual model, the fully learned model produces more stable long-horizon rollouts, tracks reverse velocity targets more reliably, and reduces hitch-angle error for better jackknife avoidance—even on sloped terrain absent from training.

Why tractor–trailers?

Reverse motion at speed makes uncertainty impossible to ignore.

Tractor–trailer systems offer a demanding, safety-critical setting for learned dynamics: the vehicle is nonlinear and underactuated, while the passive trailer can rapidly destabilize the overall system.

01

A passive trailer creates strong coupling

The tractor is actuated, but the trailer is not. Steering the tractor changes the hitch angle and trailer motion through a rigid mechanical coupling that is difficult to stabilize.

02

Reverse driving amplifies instability

In reverse, small errors in the predicted motion can compound quickly into hitch-angle growth. At higher speeds, there is less time to correct before jackknife or track departure.

03

Critical dynamics are hidden

Road friction, slope, banking, suspension behavior, and payload-dependent effects are difficult to measure online, yet they substantially change how the vehicle responds.

This makes high-speed reverse driving over snowy, sloped terrain a direct test of whether a learned dynamics model can provide useful control predictions when the analytic prior is incomplete.

03 Method

Learned rollouts for uncertain tractor–trailer control.

Our controller relies on learned state transitions that remain informative when road friction, terrain, and vehicle behavior are not directly measured.

Fully learned dynamics

An MLP consumes a fixed-length sliding window of observed states and actions, then predicts the next system transition directly from data.

Residual dynamics

A second MLP predicts only the discrepancy from the Fiala-tire prior, testing whether the analytic model improves the learned rollout.

Uncertainty-aware rollout

The learned transition model is evaluated repeatedly during control, so its ability to represent changing traction and terrain directly affects stability.

How the learned dynamics model works

Rather than receiving an explicit friction map or terrain label, the model infers hidden effects from the recent motion of the tractor–trailer.

01

Read a motion history

The input window contains recent vehicle state estimates and applied steering/acceleration commands, including the evolving hitch configuration.

02

Predict the next transition

The MLP maps that context to the next-state change, learning how traction loss, road grade, and vehicle behavior affect the response.

03

Use the model in closed loop

The learned transition is queried throughout control, so accurate terrain-sensitive prediction is essential for speed tracking and jackknife avoidance.

Analytic dynamics

A coupled tractor–trailer model with Fiala tire forces.

Alongside the learned predictor, we derive a reduced-order dynamics model that exposes the physical coupling at the hitch and provides an interpretable analytic prior.

State and configuration

The model state includes tractor position and yaw, trailer yaw, longitudinal and lateral tractor velocity, and the two yaw rates. The hitch angle is the difference between tractor and trailer yaw.

Coupled equations of motion

A rigid hitch couples tractor and trailer acceleration: tire forces, inertial effects, and body-frame motion terms are solved together at every rollout step. This coupling is what makes reverse driving especially sensitive to modeling error.

Fiala-tire analytic prior

Lateral tire forces use a Fiala brush model, with slip-angle saturation governed by road friction, normal load, and cornering stiffness. This prior is used directly for baseline control and as the physics component of the residual formulation.

01

Rigid hitch constraint

The trailer velocity is induced by tractor motion and trailer yaw rate, so accelerations cannot be solved independently.

02

Coupled rollout update

At every rollout step, the coupled acceleration system is solved and then integrated forward to update the vehicle state.

03

Why learn on top?

Road friction, slopes, suspension, and other hidden effects perturb the analytic prior—exactly the gap the learned model is designed to absorb.

Simulation settings

Reverse driving across progressively richer uncertainty.

We use the same high-speed forward and reverse racing task while progressively changing the terrain and unmodeled environmental effects seen by the controller. Snow + slope is the critical transfer setting.

01

Asphalt

Uniform high-friction, flat pavement. This nominal condition isolates the effect of unknown vehicle dynamics.

02

Snow

Noisy patches of low-friction snow interrupt an otherwise flat track, creating rapidly changing tire behavior.

03

Snow + slope

Snow patches combine with slope and banking—hidden terrain variables that are absent from the learned model’s training data and most challenging in reverse.

04 Results

High-speed reverse control under unmodeled terrain.

The fully learned model delivers dependable open-loop predictions and stronger closed-loop tracking, particularly when reverse motion meets snow, slopes, banking, and other uncertainty absent from training.
